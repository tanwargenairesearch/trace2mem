# harbor-design

## Session: harbor-design (2026-09-08)

The user asked for a migration plan for the synthetic Harbor webhook platform: inspect the system, calculate average daily payload volume and peak consumer capacity with 50% headroom, compare PostgreSQL outbox consumers with Kafka, recommend a staged architecture, and identify what cannot be costed yet [cite:harbor-0001].

The assistant inspected the system [cite:harbor-0002] [cite:harbor-0003]. Tool-reported baseline: a Go webhook receiver writes a PostgreSQL outbox row in the same transaction as the business change; three engineers; EU-only event storage; average 400 events/s with peaks of 2,400 events/s for 20 minutes; 1,500-byte payloads; measured sustainable consumer throughput of 600 events/s per replica; idempotent processing by event_id; 100 GiB free PostgreSQL storage; no Kafka expertise; EUR 900/month infrastructure ceiling with no vendor price quotes; launch target 15 November 2026; retention proposal of 30 days raw payloads and 90 days metadata [cite:harbor-0004].

Capacity tool runs with 1.5x headroom reported: at 400 events/s, 51,840,000,000 raw bytes/day (~48.3 GiB/day) requiring 1 replica; at 2,400 events/s, 311,040,000,000 raw bytes/day equivalent requiring 6 replicas. The tool noted the assumption of a constant rate over 24 hours excluding indexes, replicas, compression, and metadata [cite:harbor-0006] [cite:harbor-0007] [cite:harbor-0008] [cite:harbor-0009].

The assistant's plan concluded that 30-day raw retention (~1.45 TiB minimum) is infeasible in PostgreSQL against 100 GiB free, recommended keeping the outbox pattern while offloading raw payloads to EU object storage, autoscaling 1-6+ consumers, and deferring Kafka behind a cost go/no-go gate; it listed managed Kafka, object storage, consumer autoscaling, and Kafka training as not yet costable [cite:harbor-0010].

The user then decided: adopt PostgreSQL outbox consumers for v1, retain Go, EU-only storage, and the EUR 900/month ceiling; claim no measured cost; use six consumers as the initial peak target subject to load validation; and identify the retention risk given 100 GiB free plus a bounded backpressure and recovery plan [cite:harbor-0011]. The assistant quantified the risk (~48.3 GiB/day average raw volume; 100 GiB buffers only ~2 days; disk-full would halt business writes because the outbox write shares the business transaction) and proposed a leveled backpressure plan (alerts on lag/disk; HTTP 429 with Retry-After at low disk or sustained lag; 503 and WAL-slot mitigation at critical disk), drain math (~3,200 events/s net drain at average inflow; ~15 min for a 2-hour outage backlog; ~40 min for a full 20-minute peak backlog), replay safety via event_id idempotency, and pre-launch load-validation gates [cite:harbor-0012].

Finally, the user recorded the accepted operational policy: at-least-once processing with event_id deduplication; alert at oldest-outbox age 120 seconds; pause noncritical producers at 300 seconds; replay only after root-cause mitigation; ownership split as Mira (receiver/outbox), Leon (consumers/load testing), Saanvi (operations); and asked for an implementation sequence with acceptance checks, clearly separating unapproved recommendations [cite:harbor-0013]. The assistant produced an 8-step owner-tagged implementation sequence with acceptance checks (metric emission, alert/pause wiring, 429 pause mechanism, dedup enforcement, 6-consumer deployment and validation, failure/drain test, gated replay runbook, pre-launch sign-off) and explicitly listed unapproved items: object-storage offload, disk-based guardrails, a 7th replica, extra validation gates, the Kafka decision gate, and all costing work [cite:harbor-0014].
